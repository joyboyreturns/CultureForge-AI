### 1. `01-landing.mp4` (22.7 s)

Every business idea starts as a guess about what people in a place will love. CultureForge AI puts that guess on trial. Describe an idea, an audience and a city. We ask Qloo what that audience actually gravitates to there, then turn those signals into directions you can challenge.

### 2. `02-diagram-compare.mp4` (13.3 s)

Ask a language model about a bakery and you get fluent text with no sources. Here, every direction must point back to evidence Qloo returned.

### 3. `03-studio.mp4` (18.0 s)

Here's a real brief: an artisan bakery in Tokyo for early-morning commuters. This demo replays evidence Qloo returned earlier, so it opens instantly. Every claim traces to a real Qloo place or tag, never an invented score.

### 4. `04-places.mp4` (12.0 s)

Real places Qloo returned, with its own photos and numbers. Nothing here is scored by us: the figures are Qloo's, and the page says what it could not verify.

### 5. `05-directions.mp4` (11.0 s)

From that evidence, CultureForge composes directions. Each names the places it rests on, what it claims, and what remains unproven.

### 6. `06-diagram-pipeline.mp4` (15.3 s)

Under the hood: Qloo returns places, tags and audiences. Rules compose cited directions. An optional model step comes last, and a validator rejects any claim without a Qloo ID.

### 7. `07-map.mp4` (36.0 s)

Now the same evidence on a real map. Venues are pinned from the addresses Qloo returned, and the panel says exactly how each pin was matched. Pick a venue and see why it's connected to this audience. Then ask the hard question: what happens without it? Remove this bakery, and the directions are rebuilt from what's left, using held evidence, with no new Qloo request. What changed is shown, and what survives.

### 8. `08-diagram-verdict.mp4` (15.3 s)

A tool that always agrees is decoration, so the test has three outcomes. Both checks pass: supported. A check is missing: unproven. A check fails: contested. And you can re-anchor to another place and test again.

### 9. `09-test.mp4` (30.5 s)

Let's run it on this brief. The lead direction's anchor lands in the bottom third of the places Qloo ranked, so the verdict is contested, and the order of directions changes. It's a verdict about anchor fit in this sample, not about demand.

### 10. `10-agent.mp4` (25.7 s)

Behind it, a bounded agent plans up to eight steps over approved Qloo workflows: tags, audiences, comparisons. A step that cites anything it hasn't observed is refused, and shown as refused. The final analysis must cite Qloo IDs from this run, and says what remains unproven.

### 11. `11-diagram-layers.mp4` (13.3 s)

Three layers, kept apart: what Qloo returned, what rules decided, and what a model wrote. If the model fails or is switched off, the rule-based answer stands, and says so.

### 12. `12-close.mp4` (12.2 s)

CultureForge AI: Qloo's cultural intelligence, used as evidence that can disagree with your idea. Open source, nine Qloo workflows, and a demo you can try yourself.
