# Judge assessment and remaining sprint

Reviewed against the [2026 rubric](https://qloo.devpost.com/rules) on 21 October 2026. This is an internal product critique, not feedback from the official judges or a predicted score.

## Would this beat the previous winners?

That has not been established. Earlier entries already cover cultural business consulting and venture ideation. GeoTaste won the previous grand prize, and TAVA's published journey is close to CultureForge's original premise. See the [sourced competitive review](COMPETITIVE_REVIEW.md). Adding more dashboards or a larger model does not establish originality or superiority.

The strongest distinction is **a proposal that has to survive its evidence**: scoped falsifiers, a verdict that can disagree, alternative-anchor tests, and removal that rebuilds the actual answer. The new decision notebook connects that cultural result to a founder's next field experiment. This is a specific product argument; its usefulness still needs observation with real founders.

## What I would look for as a judge

| Question | Demonstrable answer | What remains weak |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does Qloo change the product? | Inspect provenance, remove an anchor, and compare wording, photo, sources and survival. Changes are computed from held real evidence. | A source-only change is weaker evidence of useful adaptation than an altered answer. Show the actual outcome rather than selecting a flattering label. |
| Is the agent doing a bounded, understandable job? | The research trace shows retrieval, composition and optional plan/test/reflection; the model cannot fabricate sources or set the verdict. | Optional model behavior needs rehearsal on the deployed configuration. More autonomy would require evidence that it improves decisions. |
| Can a new user get to the point? | Six-step replayable tour, visible sources, a short judge journey and direct studio links. Directions appear before deeper analysis. | Observe a first-time founder; do not assume the tour solves every point of confusion. |
| What does a founder do next? | Choose a direction, define proceed/stop criteria, record observations separately, and export a decision record. | A planning tool is not proof that a real founder benefited. The notebook deliberately starts with no claimed observations. |
| Can I trust the limits? | No invented fit scores, no mock fallback, scoped verdicts, explicit caching and unavailable states. | A deployment still needs live retrieval, adequate quota and access throughout judging. |
| Can another developer run it? | Public source, MIT license, production command, Docker packaging, tests and CI configuration. | The Docker image has not been built locally because the daemon is unavailable. |

## Highest-value work before the deadline

1. **Publish and rehearse the whole app.** Verify retrieval, failure recovery, share reopening and model configuration from a private browser. A static-only upload is insufficient.
2. **Observe real founders using it.** Use the validation protocol below. Record permission and actual observations; report limitations and critical feedback.
3. **Refine the central interaction from that evidence.** Fix where people cannot explain the difference between Qloo observations, CultureForge proposals and their own field notes.
4. **Freeze the submission materials.** Use current screenshots, the actual public URL and reproducible instructions. Confirm eligibility and media rights before submission.

Deadline in India: **31 October 2026, 9:15 am IST**. The license is mandatory under the current rules and should remain in the public repository. [Official requirements](https://qloo.devpost.com/).

## Founder validation protocol

This is a proposed study, not completed research. Recruit people who have recently considered opening, changing or programming a local venue. Ask permission before recording or quoting them. Keep private personal details out of public artifacts.

Give each person their own brief and ask them to use the studio without coaching beyond the guided tour. Observe these tasks:

- Explain one direction in their own words and identify the observation it depends on.
- State what a supported or unproven cultural verdict does and does not establish.
- Remove a reference place and explain what actually changed.
- Use the notebook to define a field experiment and a result that would make them stop or revise.

Ask: What decision were you trying to make? What did you learn that you did not have before? Which part would you distrust or ignore? What would you try next? What information is still missing?

Report participants' actual outcomes and hesitations. Treat inability to explain the verdict's scope as a product problem. Treat willingness to try another experiment as an observation, not as willingness to pay. Define any numerical study threshold before observing results and label it as your chosen evaluation criterion.

Avoid fabricated market size, testimonials, savings, demand metrics or a percentage chance of winning. A smaller demonstrated claim is more credible than an untested expansive one.
