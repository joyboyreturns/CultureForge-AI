# CultureForge: the case for a first investment

## A 60-second pitch

Hospitality brand agencies must explain why a concept belongs in a particular
place. CultureForge turns a brief into a cited cultural case they can inspect,
challenge and bring to a client review. Qloo supplies the taste signals and
places; our bounded agent investigates them. A direction can be contested,
re-tested against another place, or rebuilt after a reference is removed.
The team exports the evidence and records the next field experiment.

Our first commercial hypothesis is a $1,500, 30-day design partner pilot for
one agency team and five scoped briefs. We plan to test repeat use before
offering a capped team subscription. We have a working prototype and recorded
Qloo evidence, but no established paying customers or measured ROI. A $25,000
investment would fund commercial data access, customer validation and the
production controls needed to serve those first teams.

## What to demonstrate

1. Open a prepared brief from held real Qloo evidence. Name its acquisition
   time and distinguish replay from fresh research.
2. Follow one direction's decisions back to returned Qloo identifiers.
3. Show the evidence test. Explain its exact scope and demonstrate an actual
   contested result if the held evidence supports one; never manufacture it.
4. Remove a cited anchor. Show the description, photograph and citations
   rebuild, then explain what changed and what remained.
5. Export the brief, open the experiment notebook and define owner, review
   date, spending cap, proceed and stop criteria.
6. Open Pilot. Show the offer and download a buyer-specific discussion brief.

The distinctive moment is an answer changing when its evidence changes.
The evidence test assesses the anchor within a retrieved sample; a separate
field experiment is required to assess demand.

## Milestones after funding

| Period | Proposed evidence of progress | Decision if it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1–14 | Eight buyer interviews, commercial data quote, three scoped pilot candidates | Narrow buyer or use case; do not invent traction |
| Days 15–30 | Two paid pilots, agreed baselines, actual use in a review | Revisit offer and purchasing owner |
| Days 31–60 | One repeat purchase, observed delivery costs and documented reviewer edits | Revise pricing, scope or workflow before scaling |

These are targets. A paid pilot counts only after agreement and payment;
a repeat purchase counts only after the buyer buys again. An exported brief,
positive interview or hackathon user is not revenue.

## Questions to answer candidly

**Why does this need Qloo?** The references, cross-domain signals and anchor
checks depend on returned Qloo envelopes. Without them the system must
abstain; model prose is insufficient evidence. Removal demonstrates dependence
on held evidence, not experimentally measured superiority over all competitors.

**Why will anyone pay?** The hypothesis is that agencies repeatedly need a
defensible concept review deliverable. Test that with their own briefs and
paid continuation. We have not yet proved willingness to pay or time saved.

**What protects the business?** Workflow adoption could create repeat value;
the underlying Qloo graph is licensed, not owned. There is no demonstrated moat.

**What could kill it?** Commercial data costs above buyer value, sparse local
coverage, low repeat frequency, or reviewers finding the output too narrow.
Resolve those before broader sales or enterprise commitments.
