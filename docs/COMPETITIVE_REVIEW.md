# Competitive review — 21 October 2026

This is a comparison of published submissions, not an independent test of competitors' current deployments. Their descriptions are claims by their authors. It does not establish that CultureForge outperforms them, and it cannot estimate a winning probability.

## Positioning decision

**For independent founders deciding what experience to prototype before committing to a location, CultureForge turns Qloo cultural evidence into business hypotheses they can challenge.**

The product's advantage to demonstrate is inspectability: cited decisions, a check that can disagree, and recomposition that actually removes the withdrawn evidence. This is an implementation claim about CultureForge; it is not a claim that another project lacks those capabilities.

## Judge-facing evidence

| Area in the current rubric | Concrete proof to show | Remaining practical risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Implementation | Typed workflow builders; captured request/response provenance; bounded agent steps; cached retrieval; backend tests. | A configured key does not prove a remote service is currently available. Rehearse the hosted app. |
| Design | One path from brief to directions, verdict, removal, and export; explicit empty and failure states. | Too many optional tabs can distract. Follow the short walkthrough. |
| Impact | A founder can choose an experience to investigate and explain its assumptions. | There is no measured customer outcome or established willingness to pay. Do not invent one. |
| Idea | A cultural proposal can be contested or become unproven when its evidence changes. | It must be visibly demonstrated rather than only described. |

Source for the rubric: [2026 official rules](https://qloo.devpost.com/rules). These are qualitative readiness judgments, not judge scores.

## What would improve the entry beyond this audit

The strongest remaining evidence is a real founder trying the published product. Observe where they hesitate, whether they understand the verdict's scope, and which experiment they would run next. Report their actual feedback with permission. Do not describe a proposed user study as completed validation.
