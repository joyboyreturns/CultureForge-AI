    THE CULTUREFORGE WORKFLOW

    Find the signal.
    Then try to break it.

    CultureForge turns Qloo’s cultural graph into a local venture hypothesis. Then you can challenge its anchor, remove a place and rebuild from what remains. A direction that cannot survive its evidence should move down the page.

    01 / COMPOSE

    Make a claim from local culture.

    Each direction is a CultureForge interpretation, tied to the actual places and signals Qloo returned. The data stays evidence; it is not passed back as the answer.

    02 / CHALLENGE

    Change the place it depends on.

    Test the same direction against another returned anchor. The concept stays fixed, so you can see how much the verdict depends on that one assumption.

    03 / REMOVE

    Take evidence away.

    Remove a place and rebuild the directions, descriptions, images and anchors from what remains. See whether the idea has roots beyond one convenient example.

    A cultural stress test, not a market forecast. Verdicts are about anchor fit within the retrieved sample; they do not measure demand, competition, rent or willingness to pay.

    WHAT THE TEST ACTUALLY ASKS

    Two checks.
    Both can fail.

    A direction is a hypothesis until the local evidence has had a look at it. These are the questions, asked of the places Qloo returned.

    CHECK 01

    Where does this place sit for that profile?

    Qloo ranks the retrieved places for a demographic profile proposed from the brief. An anchor ranked last fails this policy’s relative-ranking check within the returned set.

    One Qloo call for the whole set
    CHECK 02

    What cultural tags were returned for this anchor?

    The anchor is compared with the other retrieved places. Type tags alone do not establish a distinctive cultural attribute.

    One Qloo call per direction
    THE LIMIT

    What this does not measure.

    Demand, competition, rent, footfall and willingness to pay. These checks assess a scoped anchor hypothesis within the retrieved sample, and the screen says so.

    Stated on every verdict

    The verdict is supported, contested, or unproven — by the evidence, with the reason attached.
